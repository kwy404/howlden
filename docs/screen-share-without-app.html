Screen share without an app

Your browser already knows how to share your screen. Here is how to use it with a few friends, and how to make what you share actually readable.

The screen sharing tool you already have

A lot of people think screen sharing needs a dedicated meeting app: a download, an installer, maybe a plugin that asks for permissions. On a desktop or laptop, that has not been true for years. Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari all ship with screen capture built in, exposed to web pages through a standard called the Screen Capture API.

The important detail is that the browser is in charge, not the website. A page cannot quietly grab your screen. It can only ask, and the browser shows its own picker where you decide exactly what to share, or cancel. That picker looks a little different in each browser, but the choices are the same.

howlden uses that built in feature. You open a page, join a voice channel, and share. Nothing to install on your side or your friends' side.

Step by step: sharing your screen in a den

  1. Open a den. Pick a display name and an avatar. They stay in your own browser; there is no sign up.
  2. Invite the people who should watch. Create an invite and send the link through any messenger you use, or show the QR code. Each friend opens it and sends back a short reply code, which you paste or scan. That is the whole join process.
  3. Join a voice channel together. Screen share lives inside voice channels, so everyone can talk about what they are looking at.
  4. Press screen share. Your browser's picker opens.
  5. Choose what to share and confirm. Your friends see it right away. Press the button again, or use the browser's own "stop sharing" bar, to end it.

Tab, window or entire screen: which to pick

The picker usually offers three options, and choosing well is the single biggest thing you can do for privacy and quality.

OptionGood forWatch out for
A browser tabShowing a website, a doc, a slide deck in the browserOnly that tab is visible; switching tabs does not switch the share
A windowOne app, like a code editor or a spreadsheetPop ups and menus from other apps stay hidden, which is usually what you want
Entire screenMoving between several appsEverything shows: notifications, other chats, your desktop

When in doubt, share a single window. It is the option that is hardest to regret. If you do share the entire screen, turn on your system's do not disturb mode first so a private message does not pop up in front of everyone.

Some browsers can also share the sound of a tab when you share a tab (look for a checkbox in the picker). Whether this is offered depends on your browser and operating system, so test it before you count on it for a watch party.

Making your share easy to read

A shared screen is compressed video. Small text and fine lines suffer first, especially on slow connections. A few habits help a lot:

  • Zoom in. Press Ctrl and + (or Cmd and + on a Mac) in the app you are sharing. Larger text survives compression much better.
  • Share a smaller window instead of a huge 4K desktop. Fewer pixels means each one gets more of the available bandwidth.
  • Move slowly. Fast scrolling turns into a blur for viewers. Scroll, pause, then talk.
  • Turn your camera off while sharing if the picture gets choppy. Both streams compete for your upload.
  • Use a cable or sit near the router if you can. Upload speed matters more than download speed for the person sharing.

Where your screen actually goes

In many meeting tools, your screen is uploaded to a media server once, and that server sends copies to every viewer. That approach scales well to big audiences.

howlden takes the direct route. A den is a mesh: every member has a direct connection to every other member, and your shared screen travels over those connections straight to each viewer's browser. WebRTC encrypts every connection end to end with DTLS and SRTP, and no server of ours ever sees the stream. The peer to peer chat guide explains how those connections get set up.

The trade off is upload bandwidth. With three viewers your device sends three copies; with four viewers it sends four. That is why a den holds up to 5 people, and why screen sharing with a full den works best on a decent home connection. For a lecture to fifty people, a server based meeting tool is the better fit.

Honest limits to know first

  • Phones: many phone browsers do not offer screen capture at all. On a phone you can join and watch, but sharing usually needs a desktop or laptop browser.
  • Everyone must be online at the same time. There is no recording and nothing waits on a server for latecomers.
  • Strict networks: if both you and a viewer are on strict NAT (some office, school, hotel or mobile networks), the direct connection can fail. howlden tells you when that happens and lets you add your own TURN relay in settings. See the strict NAT guide.
  • Your device is still your device. Encryption protects the stream in transit. It cannot protect a screen that someone is filming with their phone, or a computer that is already compromised.

Good moments for a quick share

Small, informal shares are where a browser tool feels best: helping a parent find a setting, walking a teammate through a bug, reviewing a school project with a study group, showing friends a build in a game before you jump in together. If you also want faces on screen, the group video call guide covers cameras in a den.

Try it in two minutes

The easiest test is with yourself: open a den on your computer, invite your phone with the QR code, join the same voice channel on both, and share a window from the computer. You will see exactly what your friends will see. When it looks right, open a den and send the invite to the people who need to watch.

Frequently asked questions

Can I share my screen without installing anything?

Yes. Modern desktop browsers have screen capture built in. In howlden you join a voice channel, press screen share, and pick a tab, a window or your whole screen in the browser's own picker.

Can I screen share from my phone's browser?

Usually not. Many phone browsers do not offer screen capture at all. You can still join from a phone and watch someone else's shared screen.

Does the other person need an account to see my screen?

No. howlden has no accounts. Your friend opens your invite link in a browser, sends back a reply code, and joins the voice channel to watch.

Who can see my shared screen?

Only the people in that voice channel of your den. The stream goes directly to their browsers over end to end encrypted WebRTC connections, not through a server of ours.
